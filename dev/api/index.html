AeroTrixi.CompressibleEulerEquationsMs1T2D — Type
CompressibleEulerEquationsMs1T2D(ref_q, mass_arr, e_int_function, c_int_function;
                                 T_min = 10.0, T_max = 3.0e4, T_tol = 1e-9,
                                 dT = 1.0, min_T_jump = 1e-6,
                                 interpolation = :linear, cv_table_offset = false)

Multicomponent compressible Euler equations in two space dimensions for a flow in thermal equilibrium, i.e. a single temperature $T$ shared by all degrees of freedom of all species:

\[\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \begin{pmatrix} \rho v_1 \\ \rho v_2 \\ \rho e_{\text{total}} \\ \rho_1 \\ \vdots \\ \rho_{n} \end{pmatrix} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \begin{pmatrix} \rho v_1^2 + p \\ \rho v_1 v_2 \\ ( \rho e_{\text{total}} + p) v_1 \\ \rho_1 v_1 \\ \vdots \\ \rho_{n} v_1 \end{pmatrix} + \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \begin{pmatrix} \rho v_1 v_2 \\ \rho v_2^2 + p \\ ( \rho e_{\text{total}} + p) v_2 \\ \rho_1 v_2 \\ \vdots \\ \rho_{n} v_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\]

Here $\rho_i$ is the density of species $i$, $\rho = \sum_{i=1}^n \rho_i$, $v_1$, $v_2$ the velocities and $e_{\text{total}}$ the specific total energy. Unlike a calorically perfect gas there is no constant $\gamma$: the pressure follows from the number density rather than from the internal energy,

\[p = n k_B T, \qquad n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\rho_i}{m_i}\]

and the internal energy is tabulated,

\[\rho e_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \rho_i e_i(T) + \frac{1}{2} \rho (v_1^2 + v_2^2)\]

with $e_i(T)$ carrying the translational, rotational and vibrational contributions of species $i$. Recovering $T$ from $e$ therefore requires a Newton iteration against the tables, which are held in a ThermoData1T instance in the thermodata field.

The conservative variables are $(\rho v_1, \rho v_2, \rho e_{\text{total}}, \rho_1, \ldots, \rho_n)$ — note that the momenta come first, there is no $\rho$ entry — and the primitive variables are $(v_1, v_2, T, \rho_1, \ldots, \rho_n)$, with $T$ in place of the pressure.

All quantities are non-dimensionalised by ref_q, so $k_B = 1$ in the equations as implemented and $p = n T$.

flux_oblapenko_etal is an entropy-conservative two-point flux for this system, available for both an orientation and a normal_direction.

Arguments

  • ref_q: ReferenceFlowQuantities (see FlowRef.jl) used to scale every quantity.
  • mass_arr: species masses in kg. Not modified.
  • e_int_function, c_int_function: one callable of T per species returning the specific internal energy and specific heat of the internal degrees of freedom; the translational parts are added internally by ThermoData1T.
  • T_min, T_max, dT: tabulation range and step, in K. Temperatures are clamped to $[1.0001\,T_{\min},\; 0.9999\,T_{\max}]$.
  • T_tol: relative tolerance of the Newton solver for $T(e)$.
  • min_T_jump: in units of dT. Below this temperature jump flux_oblapenko_etal replaces its divided differences by the equivalent midpoint expressions, which avoids the $0/0$ as $T_{rr} \to T_{ll}$.
  • interpolation: only :linear is implemented.
  • cv_table_offset: tabulate $c_v$ on a grid shifted by $-\Delta T/2$, see ThermoData1T.

References

  • Oblapenko, Torrilhon (2025). Computers and Fluids, 106640. DOI: 10.1016/j.compfluid.2025.106640
  • Oblapenko, Tarnovskiy, Ertl, Torrilhon (2024). STAB/DGLR Symposium. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-032-11115-9_36

AeroTrixi.CompressibleEulerPoissonEquations2D — Type
CompressibleEulerPoissonEquations2D(gamma; coupling = 0, rho_0 = 1)

The compressible Euler equations in 2D of a charged gas: a single charged species in a uniform neutralizing background of density $\rho_0$, under its own electrostatic potential $\phi$ and field $E = -\nabla \phi$,

\[\begin{aligned} \partial_t \rho + \nabla \cdot (\rho v) &= 0, \\ \partial_t (\rho v) + \nabla \cdot (\rho v \otimes v + p I) &= c \, \rho E, \\ \partial_t (\rho e) + \nabla \cdot ((\rho e + p) v) &= c \, \rho v \cdot E, \\ -\Delta \phi = \rho - \rho_0, \qquad E &= -\nabla \phi, \end{aligned}\]

with the coupling constant $c$ (coupling) and the background density $\rho_0$ (rho_0). The field accelerates the gas and does work on it; for $c > 0$, it pushes the gas from regions of excess density towards regions of deficit. With the charge-to-mass ratio $q/m$ of the species and the permittivity $\varepsilon_0$, the physical potential is $\phi_\mathrm{phys} = (q/m) / \varepsilon_0 \, \phi$, and

\[c = \frac{(q/m)^2}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\omega_p^2}{\rho_0},\]

with the plasma frequency $\omega_p$ of the background. A negative coupling $c = -4 \pi G$ describes self-gravity instead (attraction instead of repulsion).

The variables are $(\rho, \rho v_1, \rho v_2, \rho e, \phi, E_1, E_2)$. The Euler part is the one of CompressibleEulerEquations2D(gamma). The potential and the field have no flux and no numerical dissipation, so that they are not changed by the time integration of the DG discretization. They are set by the solution of the elliptic part of the equations, the Poisson problem, which a GridapCallback solves with continuous finite elements (see elliptic_forms). The field acts on the flow through the source terms source_terms_euler_poisson.

AeroTrixi.P4estLnodesQ2 — Type
P4estLnodesQ2(mesh::P4estMesh{2})

Continuous Q2 node numbering of the current forest of a 2D P4estMesh, including hanging nodes, their linear constraints, and boundary labels. It is derived from p4est_lnodes_new(p4est, ghost, 2) and must be rebuilt whenever the mesh changes.

The nodes of each element are numbered like the nodes of a Trixi.jl element with polydeg = 2, i.e., in lexicographic order of the reference coordinates (-1, 0, 1) with the first coordinate running fastest. All data are stored on the Julia side; the p4est structures are freed after construction. Only serial runs are supported.

Fields

  • element_nodes::Matrix{Int}: 9 × n_elements node ids of each element, where 1:n_independent are independent nodes and n_independent .+ (1:n_hanging) are hanging nodes.
  • coordinates::Matrix{Float64}: 2 × n_nodes node coordinates.
  • n_independent::Int, n_hanging::Int: number of independent and hanging nodes.
  • hanging_masters::Matrix{Int}: 3 × n_hanging independent master nodes (a, m, b) of each hanging node, where a and b are the end points of the coarse face and m is its midpoint. Each hanging node lies between a and m, at a quarter of the face.
  • hanging_coefficients::Matrix{Float64}: 3 × n_hanging constraint coefficients; the value of hanging node n_independent + k is sum(hanging_coefficients[:, k] .* values[hanging_masters[:, k]]).
  • boundary_faces::Vector{Tuple{Int, Int, Symbol}}: (element, face, name) of each element face on the boundary, with faces numbered as in p4est but one-based.
  • boundary_entities::Vector{Vector{Symbol}}: distinct sorted sets of boundary names carried by nodes. A node at the junction of two boundaries, e.g., a corner of the domain, belongs to an entity with both names.
  • node_entity::Vector{Int}: index into boundary_entities for each node, 0 for interior nodes.

See also apply_hanging_constraints!.

AeroTrixi.SurfacePointwise — Type
SurfacePointwise{Variable, NBoundaries}(boundary_symbol_or_boundary_symbols,
                                                variable, output_directory = "out")

This struct is used to compute pointwise surface values of a quantity of interest variable alongside the boundary/boundaries associated with particular names given in boundary_symbols. For instance, this can be used to compute the surface pressure coefficient SurfacePressureCoefficient or surface friction coefficient SurfaceFrictionCoefficient of e.g. an 2D airfoil with the boundary names :AirfoilTop, :AirfoilBottom which would be supplied as boundary_symbols = (:AirfoilTop, :AirfoilBottom). AeroTrixi.SurfacePointwiseCallback — Type
SurfacePointwiseCallback(semi, quantities::SurfacePointwise...; interval)

Compute and save pointwise surface quantities, such as the SurfacePressureCoefficient or the SurfaceFrictionCoefficient, at the start of the simulation, every interval time steps, and at the end. Each quantity is given as an SurfacePointwise, which determines the boundaries and the output directory; the values at all surface nodes are written to <output directory>/<variable name>_<time step>.h5, e.g., out/CP_x_000100.h5.

The quantities are computed from an evaluation of the right-hand side at the current time, which provides the solution at the boundaries and, for viscous quantities such as the friction coefficient, the gradients of the solution.

This callback replaces the keyword argument analysis_pointwise of the AnalysisCallback of AeroTrixi.jl v0.4 and is used together with the AnalysisCallback of Trixi.jl.

Example

pressure_coefficient = SurfacePointwise((:airfoil,),
                                                SurfacePressureCoefficient(p_inf, rho_inf,
                                                                           u_inf))
surface_pointwise_callback = SurfacePointwiseCallback(semi, pressure_coefficient;
                                                      interval = 100)
callbacks = CallbackSet(summary_callback, analysis_callback, surface_pointwise_callback)

AeroTrixi.ThermoData1T — Type
ThermoData1T(ref_q, mass_arr, e_c_int_function_arr;
             T_min = 10.0, T_max = 3.0e4, T_tol = 1e-9, dT = 1.0,
             interpolation = :linear, cv_table_offset = false)

Tabulated thermodynamic data for a flow of NCOMP species in thermal equilibrium, i.e. a single temperature $T$ shared by all degrees of freedom.

For each species $i$ the specific internal energy and the specific heat at constant volume are split into a translational part, which is added internally, and a part carrying the internal degrees of freedom, which is supplied by the caller:

\[e_i(T) = \frac{3}{2} \frac{k_B T}{m_i} + e_i^{\text{int}}(T), \qquad c_{v,i}(T) = \frac{3}{2} \frac{k_B}{m_i} + c_{v,i}^{\text{int}}(T)\]

Both are sampled on a uniform grid of step $\Delta T$ and evaluated by linear interpolation. The entropy integral

\[\int_{T_0}^{T} \frac{c_{v,i}(\tau)}{\tau} \, \mathrm{d}\tau\]

is accumulated over the tabulation points and completed with an exact partial-cell contribution, $T_0$ being the first point of the $c_v$ grid.

The energy table holds $N_T$ points at $T_{\min} + (i-1)\Delta T$. Where the $c_v$ table lives is set by cv_table_offset:

  • false (NoCvOffset): the same grid as the energy table.
  • true (CvOffset): a grid shifted by $-\Delta T/2$, i.e. $T_{\min} - \Delta T/2 + (i-1)\Delta T$ with $N_T + 2$ points. The two extra points keep $[T_{\min}, T_{\max}]$ strictly bracketed. Sampling $c_v$ at cell midpoints makes it consistent with the slope of the piecewise-linear energy.

Both index pairs are returned together by get_index_lower_fracpos; with an offset table they differ, and mixing them up is silently wrong.

$T(e)$ is inverted by a Newton iteration with tolerance T_tol, clamped to $[1.0001\,T_{\min},\; 0.9999\,T_{\max}]$.

All stored quantities are dimensionless, scaled by the ReferenceFlowQuantities instance ref_q, which is retained in the ref_q field.

Arguments

  • ref_q: ReferenceFlowQuantities used to non-dimensionalise every table.
  • mass_arr: species masses in kg. Not modified.
  • e_c_int_function_arr: one callable per species, (m, T) -> (e, c_v), returning the internal-degree-of-freedom contributions only. The translational parts must not be included.
  • T_min, T_max, dT: tabulation range and step, in K.
  • T_tol: relative tolerance of the Newton solver for $T(e)$.
  • interpolation: only :linear is implemented.
  • cv_table_offset: place $c_v$ on the midpoint grid, see above.

AeroTrixi.GridapCallback — Function
GridapCallback(semi; dirichlet_tags = (), interval = 1, quadrature_degree = 4)

Callback that solves the elliptic part of the equations of semi (see elliptic_forms) with continuous Q2 elements in Gridap.jl on the current mesh and writes the result into the DG solution (DG with polydeg = 2). It is called at the start of the simulation and after every interval time steps; place it after an AMRCallback in the CallbackSet, so that the solution is recomputed after mesh changes.

  • The sources elliptic_sources are evaluated at the DG nodes and transferred to the Q2 nodes with transfer_dg_to_q2!.
  • The weak forms of elliptic_forms are integrated with quadrature_degree, with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions on the boundaries named in dirichlet_tags. The linear form must be affine in the sources.
  • The solution is written into the DG solution with write_elliptic_solution and transfer_q2_to_dg!.

After each mesh change (detected with the revision counter of p4est), the callback rebuilds the node numbering, the Gridap.jl model and space, the matrix with its factorization (Cholesky if elliptic_spd, LU otherwise), and the matrices that map the source values to the load vector. A solve then only needs sparse matrix-vector products and the solve with the factorization.

With the callback alone, the solution is updated once per time step and constant during the Runge-Kutta stages, which limits the coupled time integration to first order. For the full order of the method, update it on each stage with gridap_stage_update; the callback then only acts at the start and after mesh changes.

This function requires Gridap.jl to be loaded (import Gridap).

AeroTrixi.elliptic_fields — Function
elliptic_fields(equations)

Unknowns of the elliptic part of equations as a named tuple of their spaces, e.g., (phi = Q2Field(),) for a potential. A system such as the Stokes problem would have several fields, e.g., a velocity and a pressure.

Equations with an elliptic part implement elliptic_fields, elliptic_sources, elliptic_forms, and write_elliptic_solution, and optionally elliptic_spd; a GridapCallback then solves the elliptic part with GridapCallback(semi; dirichlet_tags).

source
AeroTrixi.elliptic_forms — Function
elliptic_forms(equations, gridap)

Weak forms (a, l) of the elliptic part of equations, written with the operators of the Gridap.jl module gridap (e.g., ∫, ∇, ⋅ = gridap.∫, gridap.∇, gridap.:⋅), so that the equations do not depend on Gridap.jl:

  • a(us, vs, dΩ): bilinear form for the tuples of unknowns us and test functions vs (one entry per field of elliptic_fields),
  • l(vs, sources, dΩ): linear form with the source fields sources[k] (see elliptic_sources); it must be affine in the sources.

dΩ is the measure of the mesh. Dirichlet conditions are homogeneous, on the boundaries given to the GridapCallback.

AeroTrixi.p4est_mesh_five_trees — Method
p4est_mesh_five_trees(; l = 0.5, trees_per_block = 1, polydeg, kwargs...)

Create a 2D P4estMesh of the box [-1, 1]^2 consisting of five blocks: the square [-l, l]^2 in the middle and four trapezoids connecting its sides to the sides of the box. Each block is split into trees_per_block × trees_per_block trees. The connectivity is created with p4est_mesh_from_vertices and contains the full face and corner connectivity.

The mesh is meant as a simple workhorse for testing features of unstructured meshes:

  • trees are non-affine (bilinear) quadrilaterals,
  • the trapezoids are rotated copies of each other, so that the face numbers on both sides of a tree interface differ and some interfaces have flipped orientation,
  • the vertices (±l, ±l) are shared by three trees,
  • for trees_per_block > 1, there are vertices shared by four trees, where diagonally opposite trees only touch at a corner.

The boundary faces are named :x_neg, :x_pos, :y_neg, and :y_pos. All other keyword arguments are passed to p4est_mesh_from_vertices.

AeroTrixi.p4est_mesh_from_vertices — Method
p4est_mesh_from_vertices(vertices, tree_to_vertex; polydeg,
                         boundary_name = Returns(:boundary),
                         RealT = Float64, initial_refinement_level = 0,
                         unsaved_changes = true,
                         p4est_partition_allow_for_coarsening = true)

Create a 2D P4estMesh from an unstructured list of straight-sided quadrilateral trees.

In contrast to P4estMesh(trees_per_dimension; ...), the p4est connectivity is derived from the vertex numbering with p4est_connectivity_complete. It therefore contains the full face and corner connectivity between trees, including trees that only touch at a corner and vertices shared by an arbitrary number of trees. A complete connectivity is required by continuous node numberings such as p4est_lnodes.

Arguments

  • vertices::AbstractMatrix: 2 × n_vertices matrix of vertex coordinates.
  • tree_to_vertex::AbstractMatrix{<:Integer}: 4 × n_trees matrix of one-based vertex indices. The vertices of each tree are ordered as in p4est (z-order), i.e., (-1, -1), (1, -1), (-1, 1), (1, 1) in the reference coordinates of the tree. Each tree must be right-handed.
  • polydeg::Integer: polynomial degree used to store the geometry of the mesh.
  • boundary_name: function mapping the midpoint x::SVector{2} of a boundary face to the Symbol used as boundary name. Default: Returns(:boundary).
  • RealT::Type: the type that should be used for coordinates. Default: Float64.
  • initial_refinement_level::Integer: refine the mesh uniformly to this level before the simulation starts. Default: 0.
  • unsaved_changes::Bool: if set to true, the mesh will be saved to a mesh file. Default: true.
  • p4est_partition_allow_for_coarsening::Bool: Must be true when using AMR to make mesh adaptivity independent of domain partitioning. Default: true.

Periodicity is not supported, since it is not inherent in the list of vertices.

AeroTrixi.source_terms_euler_poisson — Method
source_terms_euler_poisson(u, x, t, equations::CompressibleEulerPoissonEquations2D)

Force of the electric field on the flow: $c \rho E$ in the momentum equations and its work $c \rho v \cdot E$ in the energy equation, with the coupling constant $c$ = equations.coupling. For a charged gas with the charge-to-mass ratio $q/m$ and the permittivity $\varepsilon_0$, $c = (q/m)^2 / \varepsilon_0 = \omega_p^2 / \rho_0$ with the plasma frequency $\omega_p$ of the background density $\rho_0$ (see CompressibleEulerPoissonEquations2D). The potential and the field have no source terms; they are set by the solution of the elliptic part of the equations.

AeroTrixi.transfer_dg_to_q2! — Method
transfer_dg_to_q2!(func, node_values, u_ode, semi, lnodes::P4estLnodesQ2)

Transfer quantities computed from the DG solution u_ode of semi (DG with polydeg = 2) to a continuous Q2 field at the nodes of lnodes.

At each DG node, func(u_node, x, equations) returns an SVector with one entry per field, e.g., SVector(u_node[1] - 1) for a single field. node_values is a matrix of size n_fields × n_nodes. Each independent node gets the average of the values of all elements sharing it. The hanging nodes then get the values of the quadratic on the coarse face they lie on (see apply_hanging_constraints!), so that the fields are continuous.

AeroTrixi.transfer_q2_to_dg! — Method
transfer_q2_to_dg!(func, u_ode, semi, lnodes::P4estLnodesQ2, node_values)

Write data computed from Q2 fields at the nodes of lnodes into the DG solution u_ode of semi (DG with polydeg = 2). node_values is a vector (a single field) or a matrix n_fields × n_nodes.

At each DG node, the new state is func(u_node, q, x, equations), where u_node is the current state and q gives access to the Q2 fields at this node with q2_value and q2_gradient. For example,

transfer_q2_to_dg!(u_ode, semi, lnodes, φ_nodes) do u, q, x, equations
    φ = q2_value(q)
    ∇φ = q2_gradient(q)
    return prim2cons(SVector(u[1], ∇φ[1], ∇φ[2], φ), equations)
end

keeps the density and sets the velocity to ∇φ and the pressure to φ.

Trixi.boundary_condition_slip_wall — Method
boundary_condition_slip_wall(u_inner, normal_direction::AbstractVector,
                             x, t,
                             surface_flux_function,
                             equations::CompressibleEulerEquationsMs1T2D)

Determine the boundary numerical surface flux for a slip wall condition. Imposes a zero normal velocity at the wall. Density is taken from the internal solution state and pressure is computed as an exact solution of a 1D Riemann problem. Further details about this boundary state are available in the paper:

  • J. J. W. van der Vegt and H. van der Ven (2002) Slip flow boundary conditions in discontinuous Galerkin discretizations of the Euler equations of gas dynamics PDF

Details about the 1D pressure Riemann solution can be found in Section 6.3.3 of the book

  • Eleuterio F. Toro (2009) Riemann Solvers and Numerical Methods for Fluid Dynamics: A Practical Introduction 3rd edition DOI: 10.1007/b79761

The implementation is modified to ensure non-negativity of p_star. This modification preserves entropy stability based on the analysis in the paper:

  • F. J. Hindenlang, G. J. Gassner, D. A. Kopriva (2020) Stability of wall boundary condition procedures for discontinuous Galerkin spectral element approximations of the compressible Euler equations DOI: 10.1007/978-3-030-39647-3_1

Should be used together with UnstructuredMesh2D, P4estMesh, or T8codeMesh.
